What are we actually solving?

The Knapsack Problem is a combinatorial optimization problem. You get a collection of items, each with a weight and a value, and a bag that can only carry so much. Pick the items that give the highest total value without going over the weight limit.

Pack the bag yourself

Why is this optimization? There are 2n ways to choose from n items, and most of them are either too heavy or leave value on the table. We want the single best one, not just any one that fits.

Two flavours of the same idea

0/1 Knapsack

Every item is taken whole or left behind. It cannot be cut. Solved with Dynamic Programming or Branch and Bound. The goal is the highest value within capacity.

Fractional Knapsack

Items can be split, so you can take part of one. The Greedy Algorithm works: sort by value-to-weight ratio and fill the bag in that order.

Dynamic programming, one cell at a time

Step 1: Define the subproblemdp[i][w] = best value using the first i items with capacity w
Step 2: Recurrence relationif weight[i] > w: dp[i][w] = dp[i-1][w] otherwise: dp[i][w] = max( dp[i-1][w], value[i] + dp[i-1][w-weight[i]] )
Step 3: Initializationdp[0][w] = 0 dp[i][0] = 0
Step 4: Final resultanswer = dp[n][W] n = number of items, W = capacity

Yellow is the cell being computed. Orange cells are the two it looks at. A green outline marks the final answer.

Three ways to attack it

Brute Force

Try every subset and keep the best one that fits.

Good: simple and always correct.
Bad: explodes quickly as items grow.

Optimal O(2ⁿ)

Greedy Algorithm

Sort by value-to-weight ratio, add whatever fits.

Good: very fast, exact for the fractional version.
Bad: can miss the best answer for 0/1.

Not optimal for 0/1 O(n log n)

Dynamic Programming

Build answers for smaller bags and items, then combine them.

Good: exact and polynomial in n and W.
Bad: needs a table, slow when W is huge.

Optimal O(nW)
